Platform Baseline internal

Addresses and hardware boundaries used by the first smoke test.

Confirmed baseline

The target CPU is the MC6803, the video device is the MC6847, and the software-oriented screen buffer is `$4000-$41FF`. The first executable is placed at `$5000`.

The physical MCX-128 map assigns `$BF00` bit 0 to P0, which selects the bank group for `$0000-$3FFF` and `$C000-$FFFF`, and bit 1 to P1, which selects the bank group for `$4000-$BFFF`. `$BF01` bits 0-1 select the four ROM/RAM map modes.

The smoke test selects all-RAM mode and verifies distinct signatures through all eight selectable 16K RAM windows. Its P1 routine is copied to `$D000` first, so P1 can change without remapping the executable at `$5000` or overwriting the P0 signature at `$C000`.

The keyboard is an active-low eight-column matrix. Port 1 selects one column with a low bit; `$BFFF` returns the six ordinary row bits, and Port 2 bit 1 carries the Control, Break, and Shift line. The stock ROM scans all columns and confirms changes after a debounce delay.

The initial NTSC timing model is 228 master-clock periods per line, 262 lines per field, approximately 59.923 Hz, and 14,934 MC6803 E-clock periods per field. The MC6847 has an FS field-sync output, but no verified route from FS to a periodic MC6803 IRQ/NMI source has been identified. The executable includes a timer-compare test that reports 60 predicted fields and toggles P2.0 for external FS capture. The figures are derived from the service-manual clock and current MC6847 emulator timing, not yet a physical measurement.

Source boundary

The physical map is recorded in the MCX-128 register-map document, based on the MCX128 Hardware Info document and cross-checked against XRoar's mcx128.c, the MC-10 ROM disassembly, and an independent MC10 emulator.

Physical reset behavior, `$0014` direct-page selection, EPROM boot behavior, bank ownership during cassette loading, alpha-mode values, keyboard electrical validation, and frame/timer measurement remain open; see the timer-compare procedure. XRoar documents MC-10 support as unfinished and unsupported. Current MAME source also implements an MCX-128 device and is the planned independent emulator cross-check.

Detailed source inventory is in docs/research.md.
